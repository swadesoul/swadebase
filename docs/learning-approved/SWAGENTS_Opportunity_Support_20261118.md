# SWAGENTS Opportunity & Support

## Working blueprint — November 18, 2026 (America/Chicago)

**Status:** Proposed operating structure, supported by researched program information. This document is not evidence that outbound calling, email delivery, applications, referrals, or employment placement are connected or activated.

**Purpose:** Give a person one understandable place to get help, identify their strengths, find suitable opportunities, prepare accurate materials, authorize useful action, and follow progress through to an actual outcome. Support Black-owned businesses, veterans, elders, caregivers, people experiencing homelessness, and other people who need assistance without making assumptions about their needs or abilities.

The customer works with one Personal Advocate. That Advocate coordinates specialists and keeps one shared action plan. The specialist roles can reuse SWADEBASE services and existing SWAGENTS characters; eight responsibilities do not require eight new platforms or continuously running paid models.

## 2. One person, one action plan

The person should be able to say: "I need help with money," "I need work," "I don't know how to explain what I can do," "I want customers," or "I'm helping someone else."

The Personal Advocate starts with the person's priority. It asks about location and only the practical details needed for the next step. A person can discover resources without supplying a Social Security number, bank details, full medical history, or identity documents.

The plan tracks:

- The person's chosen outcome and time horizon.
- Urgent barriers such as food, essential services, transport, communication access, housing, caregiving, or an approaching notice deadline.
- Skills, work preferences, availability, and actual constraints the person chooses to share.
- The current owner and next action for each task.
- The exact authority to research, draft, contact, submit, or accept terms.
- Required documents and their private access permissions.
- Source evidence, verification date, and uncertainty.
- Response or appointment status and provider receipts.
- What the person actually received, earned, saved, or completed.

Support and employment can proceed together where practical. Do not make housing assistance conditional on entrepreneurship or a paid plan. Do not assume everyone needs the same sequence.

## 3. The Local Resource Scout's work contract

The Scout receives a need and a location, not the person's whole private file. State alone may be insufficient; city names can repeat and ZIP codes can cross service boundaries. County or the provider's actual service area must be confirmed where it matters.

For every candidate resource, record the organization, program, official source URL, service area, contact number, relevant hours, contact method, eligibility questions, necessary documents, application channel, deadlines, and the time and method of the check. Keep these facts separate:

1. The official source was found.
2. The source page was reached during this check.
3. The phone number is printed on that source.
4. The organization serves this location and relevant population.
5. Intake or applications are currently open.
6. Funding, seats, or service capacity were actually confirmed.
7. A person or authorized agent reached the provider.
8. The provider accepted the referral or application.
9. The person received the service or a decision.

Unknown facts remain unknown. A Source checked label is different from Provider confirmed. Stale information should be rechecked before consequential action. A failed website fetch is not proof that the program has closed.

Sources are program information, never instructions to change agent permissions, disclose documents, or make payments. The Scout should use bounded, allowlisted requests and the existing directory and maps infrastructure where authorized. It should not silently add cross-system data privileges or build a competing GroundLift directory.

## 4. Turning lived experience into a credible skills profile

The Skills & Career Coach interviews the person in ordinary language: What have people relied on you to do? What did you handle regularly? What tools did you use? What problems did you solve? What would you like to keep doing? What schedule and working conditions fit your life?

For each skill, keep the person's explanation, a concrete example, relevant dates if known, and the type of evidence. A person who coordinated a community event may have scheduling or supplier coordination experience, but the agent must ask what they actually did before claiming it. Caregiving can involve real organizational experience; it must not be represented as a licensed clinical qualification.

Outputs can include a one-page skills profile, a 30-second introduction, a resume, a short biography, work samples, and a service description. The person approves the wording and controls which personal circumstances are disclosed.

Career paths should distinguish work they can pursue now, work requiring a small verified training step, and longer-term goals. Consider ordinary employment, part-time work, paid apprenticeships, self-employment, and service businesses according to the person's choices. Do not promise admission, income, or a job.

The Department of Labor describes American Job Centers as sources of career counseling, training referrals, job listings, and related employment assistance. Registered Apprenticeship combines paid work experience, mentorship, instruction, progressive wages, and a recognized credential; applications go to the actual employer or sponsor. Local service availability and current openings must still be verified.

Sources:

- https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Skills/skills-matcher.aspx

## 5. Employment journey example

**Illustrative person:** Denise is 61, previously worked in retail, organized community events, and took time away from paid work to help a family member. She wants stable part-time work and needs a reachable work location.

1. The Advocate asks what she wants and identifies schedule, transport, and income priorities without assuming her age or caregiving history makes her unable to work.
2. The Skills Coach records the tasks she actually performed and checks which skills she wants to use.
3. The Scout finds specific current roles, an American Job Center, and relevant training options in her verified area. SCSEP can be considered only against its unemployment, age, income, and other actual rules; it is one possible route.
4. The Documents Specialist prepares an accurate resume and introduction. It does not invent sales figures, references, licenses, or employment dates.
5. The reviewer checks that the openings still exist, the materials are truthful, and the proposed sharing is authorized.
6. The Outreach Specialist uses the employer's accepted application process and approved contact channel. It may ask whether applications are open and arrange a conversation; Denise participates in her own interview.
7. The Coach practices interview questions. The Advocate coordinates any practical support the person chooses.
8. Follow-through records whether an interview occurred, an offer was received, work began, and whether support is needed after starting. Job-offer, job-start, and retained-employment are separate outcomes.

## 6. Business and community pathways

For a Black-owned business, activate Business & Visibility, the Scout, Documents, Outreach, and Money as needed. Start with the actual offer, delivery capacity, customer need, proof of work, and follow-up. Measure inquiries, appointments, paid work, repeat customers, and cost. Owner identity can be visible by choice; private financial, housing, veteran, or health information stays outside promotional materials unless specifically authorized and necessary.

For veterans, translate verified service experience into civilian skills and check applicable programs. Do not assume disability, unemployment, or housing instability. Benefit claims representation belongs with appropriately accredited people.

For elders, provide clear text, large controls, a phone option, plain explanations, optional repetition, and trusted-helper permissions. Ask whether they want a family member involved; age alone does not grant anyone else control.

For people experiencing homelessness, offer a low-friction route to existing help and practical employment support. Ask about safe communication and contact preferences. Never display confidential shelter locations or treat a public directory as proof of a bed or immediate placement.

For caregivers, check caregiver support separately from the care recipient's other benefits. A denial from one program does not decide all other programs.

## 7. Calling, email, and authority

One shared communications service should support all roles. Each outward-facing agent has its own role identity while authorization is attached to a specific customer, organization, task, and time period.

A proposed employer call opening is: "I'm an AI assistant with SWAGENTS, calling with Denise's permission to ask about your part-time openings and application process. Is this the right contact?"

A proposed benefits call opening is: "I'm an AI assistant helping this person find local support, with their permission. Before discussing private information, what authorization do you need, and which programs can your office screen for?"

The customer can authorize a limited set of actions and recipients in advance. The agent may work within that scope without requesting approval for every sentence. New recipients, unapproved sharing, material commitments, payments, changes outside the agreed limits, and legally restricted representation require the appropriate human decision.

Caller identity must be honest. Provider verification, recipient consent where required, applicable calling rules, recording permissions, and clear transfer options belong in the workflow. If an organization requires the person or an authorized professional to participate, the system arranges that handoff. No silent identity substitution.

Suggested authorization fields: principal, permitted helper, task, recipient or approved organization set, allowed actions, allowed data, prohibited commitments, spending/time/attempt limits, expiry, revocation state, and confirmation receipt.

## 8. Quality, privacy, and real outcomes

The independent reviewer checks high-impact outputs before external action. Routine tasks can use deterministic checks. Financial, legal, medical eligibility, credentials, and disputed representations need appropriate expert escalation. The reviewer may return a task for correction, but cannot authorize a protected action on the person's behalf.

Keep self-reported information, official-source facts, provider-confirmed details, application receipts, and actual outcomes visibly distinct. Do not create a score that rates a person's worthiness. Match services to relevant requirements and the person's preferences.

Success measures include completed appointments, submitted applications with receipts, received benefits or services, verified bill reductions after fees, interviews attended, jobs started, continued employment, and business revenue actually received. Calls placed, resumes generated, and lists of links are intermediate activity measures.

Track denial reasons and review rights from the actual notice and governing program. There is no universal 30-day appeal deadline. Keep the notice date, receipt date when relevant, deadline source, unresolved ambiguity, and next owner. Uncertain or urgent deadlines should be checked with the agency or a qualified advocate.

## 9. Existing ecosystem reuse and current limits

Read-only inspection identified SWADEBASE identity, permissions, storage, adapters, and a shared knowledge index; SSS Brain for knowledge and supervision; SWAGENTS agent conversations; DNA Front as the documented phone service route; existing mail infrastructure; GroundLift's directory and referral data model; and Swade Maps for geographic lookup.

The inspected SWAGENTS agent chat does not currently retrieve general shared-index documents or execute Scout research automatically. The inspected phone and worker readiness remains incomplete. A stored document is not proof that customer agents can make calls or submit applications. A proposed role address is not a provisioned and tested mailbox.

GroundLift already has source and verification fields suitable for a resource directory. The inspected public nonconfidential coverage is limited and dated; it does not establish fresh nationwide coverage. SWAGENTS has no established permission to read that schema directly. Reuse requires a scoped adapter exposing only authorized public information, preserving confidential records.

Swade Maps is a geographic lookup service. It does not determine benefits eligibility, program funding, or current phone availability. No generic live web-research worker was established by this inspection.

### 1. Local aging and benefits assistance

Find the local aging agency and the right office for an older adult's or caregiver's needs.

Reference ID: `eldercare-local-aging`. Source framework reviewed 2026-11-18 (America/Chicago); local availability requires confirmation.


**What must be checked**

- Agencies serve defined areas; names and services vary locally.

- A referral does not establish eligibility, funding, or an available appointment.


**Intake questions**

- What city, state, or ZIP code should we search?

- What help is most important right now?

- Would you prefer a call, text, online help, or assistance from someone you trust?


**Documents to ask the program about**

- Initial referral: location and a description of the need; confirm before sending documents.


**Questions for the provider**

- Do you serve this location and need?

- What is available now, and what are the application requirements?

### 4. Medicaid spouse and estate questions

Reference ID: `medicaid-spouse-estate`. Source framework reviewed 2026-11-18 (America/Chicago); local availability requires confirmation.

## Transcript corrections and operational cautions

1. Informational referral draft: sources were reviewed for broad federal or example-state frameworks. Local availability, intake, staffing, costs, funding, and individual eligibility remain unverified and must be confirmed.

2. A possible match is not a decision. Distinguish screening, preparation, submitted-with-receipt, agency review, decision, and service actually received.

3. Do not promise money waiting, guaranteed approval, or that knowing a program name is enough to receive assistance.

4. Do not claim every county has every program, every service is free, or age alone establishes eligibility.

5. Paid-family-caregiver rules depend on state, program, relationship, service authorization, enrollment, and effective date. Not every relative or spouse can be paid.

6. Waiting lists are not universally first come, first served; priority can reflect urgency, transitions, need, or other rules.

7. Do not impose a universal 30-day appeal deadline. Read the actual notice and confirm the trigger date, submission route, deadline, and any separate continued-benefit deadline.

8. Do not say benefits are never retroactive or always start at approval. Application-date and retroactivity rules differ. Coverage retroactivity does not automatically entitle a caregiver to back pay.

9. Medicaid retroactive-eligibility rules change for applications on or after January 1, 2027. Check current CMS guidance, state implementation, eligibility group, and application date.

10. Do not repeat an undated approximately-30-states claim. Historic national participation totals do not establish current local availability.

11. Do not hard-code annual income, resource, premium, stipend, award, or tax thresholds without an effective date and current verification.

12. Do not claim most denials are paperwork errors without evidence. Preserve the actual reason and distinguish missing information from substantive eligibility issues.

13. Clinical assessments and eligibility decisions belong to the responsible program and qualified professionals, not an intake assistant.

14. Initial discovery should not require Social Security numbers, full medical histories, complete financial records, or identity-document uploads. Collect and share sensitive information later only for a stated need with appropriate permission.

15. Outbound calls require permission, defined limits, accurate AI-assistant identification, and compliance with the receiving organization's authorization rules. Do not impersonate the person or a professional.

16. A possible match does not authorize insurance changes, asset transfers, signatures, settlements, charges, or sensitive-record disclosures.

17. PACE, Medicaid, VA assistance, and other programs have separate eligibility and coordination rules. One program's decision does not decide all others.

18. The USDA SNAP summary flags pending legal updates. USDA pages were verified by official search retrieval rather than successful direct access; confirm current state instructions before operational eligibility calculations.

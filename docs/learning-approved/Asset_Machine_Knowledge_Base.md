## Purpose and scope

Asset Machine helps a person move from an asset claim to an organized, evidence-supported review. This knowledge base covers bonds, Safe Keeping Receipts or safekeeping receipts (SKRs), standby letters of credit (SBLCs), inherited documents and property, gold, gemstones, digital wallets and tokenization. It teaches users what to collect, which institution or specialist can verify a particular fact, and how to describe the result without overstating it.

Research date: November 3, 2026. Primary focus: United States, with international banking, custody and digital-asset considerations. A rule that applies to one jurisdiction or instrument must not be generalized to another. The operational checklists are Asset Machine's recommended review procedures; they are not representations that a regulator has approved this platform or that an institution will accept an asset.

An asset review is a collection of scoped findings. A genuine object can have disputed ownership. A valid security can be nontransferable, in default or already redeemed. A legitimate bank undertaking can be conditional and unsuitable as collateral for the proposed transaction. A token can exist while the claimed underlying property does not. The appropriate conclusion depends on the exact question and evidence.

The word "proof" in this library means evidence supporting a specified claim at a specified time. It does not mean a universal guarantee, insurance policy, legal judgment or promise of liquidity. Asset-specific legal, tax, banking, valuation and laboratory decisions belong to appropriately qualified professionals and institutions.

## F01 The six questions behind an asset claim

| Question | What the reviewer is trying to establish | Examples of relevant evidence | What does not settle it alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existence and identity | The particular obligation, property or ledger entry exists and matches the description | Issuer records; inspection tied to serial numbers; lab examination; correct-chain ledger observations | A picture, document title, ticker symbol or copied serial number |
| Ownership and authority | The claimant holds the relevant legal right or has authority to act for its holder | Chain of title; account registration; estate authority; mandate; beneficial ownership and entity records | Possession, being an heir, a corporate title or control of a wallet key |
| Custody and control | Who holds the asset, what can be released, and under whose instructions | Independently confirmed custody statement; inventory; release terms; bounded wallet-control evidence | A storage receipt without verification, a dashboard or a broker's assurance |
| Value | A specified kind of value at a stated date, with a defensible method | Market observations, independent appraisal, executed trades, assay-based inputs, redemption calculation | Face amount, insurance value, asking price or issuer-defined token price |
| Transferability and enforceability | Whether the proposed right can be sold, assigned, pledged, redeemed or enforced | Contract terms; registry status; restrictions; consents; qualified jurisdiction-specific legal review | "Transferable" in an advertisement or an unrestricted token-transfer function |
| Actual market acceptance | Whether an identified buyer, lender or counterparty will proceed on defined terms | Dated, authenticated institution-specific acceptance or binding commitment, with conditions understood | An introduction, generic lender list, indicative term sheet or claimed advance percentage |

Use an additional cross-cutting review for restrictions and adverse claims: liens, competing ownership, sanctions, litigation, export controls, court orders, contractual lockups and insolvency exposure. Which searches are necessary depends on the asset, location, parties and proposed activity. A successful search of one database cannot establish the absence of every possible claim.

These six questions form Asset Machine's analytical framework. They should appear as separate evidence states in a case report. They must not be combined into an unexplained "98 percent authentic" badge. A high number of submitted pages cannot compensate for missing issuer confirmation or disputed title.

## F02 What different kinds of proof actually establish

| Evidence item | Appropriate use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Photograph or scan | Record visible wording, markings and condition | Does not establish genuine issuance, undisclosed alterations, possession today or title |
| OCR output | Make submitted text searchable | Extraction can misread zeros, dates, decimal places, names and currency symbols; compare critical fields with the original |
| File hash | Check whether later bytes match a recorded file | Identical bytes can contain false statements; a hash does not authenticate the asset |
| Digital signature | Support attribution to a signing key under a verified process | Identity, authority, compromise, certificate status and the scope of the signed claim still matter |
| Notarial act | Evidence of the particular notarial act performed | Read the certificate and local law; do not interpret notarization as financial, material or title authentication |
| Apostille | Authenticate specified origin features of an eligible public document | The Hague Convention concerns signatures, official capacity and seals; it does not establish an asset's value or the truth of its economic claims [F01] |
| Institutional confirmation | Establish only the facts the authorized institution expressly confirms | A bank's existence or an employee's existence does not authenticate a particular instrument |
| Laboratory report | Establish the findings about the item/sample examined under the stated methods | Report authenticity, item matching, scope, sampling and custody must be checked separately |
| Legal opinion | Provide a professional analysis within its stated assumptions and jurisdiction | It is not an assay, bank commitment, title insurance policy or universally binding judgment |
| Appraisal | State a value conclusion for a defined purpose, date and market | It is not a guaranteed sale price or a financing commitment |
| Audit or attestation | Report the work performed, criteria, period and conclusion | The name of the engagement does not remove limitations or extend it to facts outside its scope |
| Ledger entry | Show network-recorded activity under the relevant chain's rules | Off-chain descriptions, legal rights and the identity of a key holder require additional evidence [F03] [F04] |

Blockchain can make later alteration of recorded information more detectable, but physical-world accuracy still depends on the information supplied and its verification. NIST describes the ledger as tamper evident and tamper resistant. A Federal Reserve staff workshop publication explicitly distinguishes protection of recorded data from verification of external facts. These are technical boundaries, not a judgment on any particular asset. [F03] [F04]

## F03 A repeatable verification procedure

### Step 1 Record the claim exactly

Ask: "What do you believe you own, what right do you want to exercise, and what outcome are you seeking?" Preserve the claimant's words separately from the reviewer's classification. Record the claimed issuer, owner, location, amount, currency, dates and identifiers. If an unfamiliar nickname is supplied, keep it as a label pending classification rather than inventing a financial product.

Distinguish the user's goal: identification, estate administration, insurance, sale, redemption, borrowing, collateral review, custody, transfer or tokenization. The evidence needed to sell a collectible certificate differs from evidence needed to claim that its printed face amount remains payable.

### Step 2 Preserve the originals and intake trail

Assign a case identifier and evidence identifiers. Record who provided each item, when, how and in what capacity. Preserve the original file before OCR, redaction or translation. Keep derived copies linked to their original. For physical property, record custody transfers and packaging changes; do not arrange destructive testing without appropriate authority and a documented plan.

Do not ask users to place private keys, wallet recovery phrases, passwords or one-time access codes in the case record. Keep bank details, identity documents, estate records and private legal work in the protected workspace. Public examples should use invented names and synthetic identifiers.

### Step 3 Classify the asset and the relevant jurisdictions

Identify the asset's location, the claimant's capacity, the issuer or obligor, the account or registry, and the governing documents. For cross-border matters, record separately the law governing title, the law governing the contract, the place of custody, and any market in which an offering or transfer is proposed. Do not infer governing law solely from the currency used.

### Step 4 Build an evidence gap list

For every important claim, specify the missing evidence and who could supply it. For example: "The receipt names 12 bars; a current bar list and confirmation directly from the vault remain missing." Avoid requests such as "send proof of funds" without defining the fact to be established and a secure method.

### Step 5 Establish an independent confirmation channel

Find the relevant institution through its regulator, official registry or independently obtained official site. Verify the department and representative's authority through that channel. For institutions claiming U.S. FDIC insurance, BankFind is one useful identity and website check; it is not an instrument-validation service or a global list of legitimate financial institutions. The FDIC warns that criminals copy bank branding and create fake sites. [F02]

Do not use only a telephone number printed on the questioned document, a contact supplied by the intermediary, a QR code, or a link in an unsolicited message. Obtain the customer's consent or mandate where needed. A bank may properly decline to disclose information without authorization; that refusal alone does not establish fraud.

### Step 6 Obtain asset-specific verification

Use the specialist pathway in the relevant module. Reconcile exact names, identifiers, dates, units and amounts across records. Ask who examined the original, who selected any sample, who held the asset before and after inspection, and which institution stands behind the finding.

Record qualified findings precisely. "The laboratory confirmed issuing report R, and the stone examined matches its recorded characteristics" is more useful than "certified genuine." "The custodian confirmed a position as of 10:00 UTC, subject to a pledge" is more useful than "proof of funds passed."

### Step 7 Review rights, restrictions and valuation separately

Identify who can authorize the proposed transaction and what consents are required. Resolve discrepancies before assuming that an assignable claim is equivalent to a transferable asset. Select the correct valuation purpose, date, currency and unit. Obtain the independent professional review appropriate to material uncertainty.

### Step 8 Record a disposition with unresolved items

Each result should say what was checked, by whom, against what evidence, when, and with what limitations. Name the next responsible party. If an institution's answer contradicts a submitted document, preserve both records and escalate. Do not quietly replace the adverse response with a more favorable broker statement.

### Step 9 Refresh before reliance

Ownership, pledges, balances, terms, legal authority and market conditions can change. Select a refresh period appropriate to the fact and transaction. A wallet observation may become stale immediately; an old physical inspection may remain historically useful while requiring a fresh custody confirmation. A fixed 30-day validity rule is not universally appropriate.

## F04 Evidence states and release decisions

These are recommended product states, not statutory classifications.

| State | Meaning | Suitable next action |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted | A claimant provided the item or statement | Preserve and classify |
| Extracted | Text or fields were extracted and important fields compared | Request missing context |
| Source matched | A record or report reference was located at its purported source | Verify the exact item and authorized holder |
| Independently confirmed | An authorized independent source confirmed specified facts | Record scope, time and limitations |
| Specialist reviewed | A named qualified specialist completed a defined review | Resolve conditions and any adverse findings |
| Pending | Required evidence or review is missing | Name the missing item and responsible party |
| Disputed | Material sources conflict or an adverse claim is unresolved | Hold the affected transaction for review |
| Unable to verify | Reasonable scoped verification did not establish the claim | Describe the failed checks; do not infer fraud automatically |
| Contradicted | Reliable evidence conflicts with the specified claim | Preserve the evidence and obtain qualified review |
| Expired or superseded | A prior finding is no longer current or a new version replaces it | Obtain an updated finding |

"Transaction ready" must be a separate, transaction-specific decision. It requires the relevant parties' actual conditions to be satisfied. "Ready for legal review" and "ready to lend against" describe different milestones. A reviewer may approve a report that clearly states the asset remains unverified; report approval must not silently become asset approval.

Use explicit fields such as `existence_status`, `ownership_status`, `custody_status`, `valuation_status`, `transferability_status` and `counterparty_acceptance_status`. Each needs evidence references. A default should be `unknown` or `pending`, never `verified`.

## F05 Fraud patterns and appropriate responses

The following are prompts for investigation, not automatic accusations: unusually large value without a verifiable issuer record; pressure to pay before independent contact; a demand for secrecy about the institution supposedly responsible; inconsistent serials or names; refusal to identify the actual custodian; unverifiable legal or audit credentials; guaranteed financing percentages; and requests to sign wallet transactions described as identity checks.

Treasury identifies specific counterfeit historical notes and bonds, including supposed instruments in $100 million or $500 million denominations that it did not issue in the alleged period. Exact comparison with the published warning is appropriate; the denomination by itself is not a universal test for every modern asset or debt position. [F05]

The SEC warns that purported secret "prime bank" high-yield investment programs are scams. Such schemes misuse legitimate terms including SBLC and bank guarantee. A legitimate standby used to support a commercial obligation must be assessed on its own terms; the existence of genuine banking terminology does not validate a promised trading program. [F06]

Separate normal professional costs from suspect payment demands. Genuine lawyers, laboratories, couriers and institutions may charge documented fees. Investigate who is providing the service, the exact deliverable, payment instructions, refund terms and whether the service can actually resolve the stated uncertainty. A fee to "activate" secret Treasury funds or unlock a fictional account is a different proposition from a laboratory's written examination engagement.

If a suspicious matter arises, preserve records and stop the specific action that would increase exposure. Use the institution's independently obtained fraud channel and obtain appropriate advice. Do not post allegations about named individuals in a public knowledge base. Do not send originals, money or credentials to purported recovery services based solely on their promise to recover losses.

## F06 How to evaluate an expert or verifying organization

Choose the professional for the question. A gemologist does not decide probate authority; an attorney does not establish gold fineness through a document review; a blockchain analyst does not prove an off-chain custodian has unpledged reserves; and a broker's statement of interest is not a lender's credit approval.

Request the legal entity name, official contact route, scope of practice or license where applicable, qualifications, engagement terms, conflicts, liability limitations and exactly what report will be produced. Ask whether the engagement includes the original item, a sample, a copy or records only. Verify the report directly with its issuer when authorized.

Independence is more than a separate logo. Disclose referral payments, success fees, common ownership, who selected the expert and who paid. A paid engagement can still be independent, but compensation that depends on an unusually high value or successful financing can create a conflict that needs attention.

Avoid an invented hierarchy in which every "certificate" outranks every "letter." Weight the actual source, method, scope, authority, date, conflicts and corroboration. A brief direct response from an authorized issuer may resolve a specific question more effectively than a lengthy unsupported report.

## F07 A practical asset review file

For each case, maintain a factual summary, asset inventory, claimant and authority record, evidence index, independent confirmation log, ownership and restrictions analysis, valuation record, outstanding questions and a versioned review result. Keep legal working notes separate from client-visible reports according to existing permissions.

The confirmation log should record the institution and department, how its contact details were independently located, the consent or authority used, the questions asked, the exact facts confirmed, the response date and any qualifications. Keep no-response and declined-to-disclose results distinct from contradiction.

The evidence index should preserve the original filename, file integrity hash where used, submitter, acquisition date, issuing institution, document date, version, confidentiality level and linked asset identifiers. Original evidence and reviewer interpretation should remain distinguishable. Corrections to OCR must not overwrite the source image.

The valuation record should identify the valuation purpose, gross and net amounts, currency, units, valuation date, price source or professional, assumptions, liquidity limits and costs. "Unknown" is acceptable; inventing a plausible price is not. Keep indicative offers, executed sales and appraisals in separate fields.

## F08 Questions Asset Machine should ask first

1. What exactly is the item or right, using the wording on the record?
2. Are you the owner, executor, trustee, beneficiary, authorized representative, buyer or intermediary?
3. What outcome do you want: identification, inheritance transfer, sale, redemption, financing or tokenization?
4. Who issued the obligation, holds the property, maintains the account or operates the relevant registry?
5. Which country and state govern the relevant property, account or agreement?
6. What original records, independent confirmations or physical examinations already exist?
7. Is there any pledge, dispute, missing original, changed owner, pending probate or restriction?
8. Has anyone requested money, secrecy, account access or a wallet signature as a condition of checking the claim?

These questions should lead to the correct module and a bounded evidence request. They are not a substitute for the specialist review itself.

# Bonds, safekeeping receipts, and standby letters of credit

**Asset Machine knowledge base | Research date November 3, 2026**

A bond, a safekeeping receipt, and a standby letter of credit represent different kinds of rights. Identifying the document correctly is the first step toward deciding whether an asset exists, who controls it, and what someone can legally do with it.

This module provides an educational review process. The checklists and examples are Asset Machine's suggested workflow. They are not statements that every institution must accept a document or that every transaction follows one jurisdiction's law. Source references identify the authorities supporting specific legal, banking, and market points. No individual asset has been submitted or authenticated here.

## 1. Identify the claim before evaluating its value

| Item presented | Start with this question | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Bond certificate or securities statement | What debt obligation is identified, and is it still outstanding? | Issuer or registrar confirmation, holding records, ownership authority, restrictions, and current pricing |
| Safekeeping receipt, often abbreviated SKR | What exactly did the custodian acknowledge receiving? | Custody agreement, identified inventory, current custody confirmation, release rights, and encumbrances |
| Standby letter of credit, or SBLC | Who has promised to honor which presentation, for whose benefit, and until when? | Bank authentication, complete operative terms, amendments, beneficiary rights, and presentation requirements |
| Offer to monetize any of these | What transaction will produce money, and who has agreed to fund it? | Named buyer or lender, written conditions, accepted collateral, authorized transfer, and settlement arrangements |

A document can be authentic while the proposed conclusion is wrong. A real receipt may describe a sealed package whose contents were never examined. A genuine bond may have been paid. A valid standby may expire before the proposed financing closes. Treat each proposition as a separate question and record the evidence that answers it.

## 2. Bonds: establish the obligation and the holding

### What a bond represents

A bond generally represents money lent to an issuer under stated repayment terms. Principal, interest, maturity, credit quality, and the issuer's ability to pay matter. Investors can lose money when an issuer defaults or when a bond is sold for less than its purchase price. A stated face amount is not a promise that a buyer will pay that amount today. [B15]

FINRA identifies credit, interest-rate, liquidity, call, and other risks as relevant to bond due diligence. Read the actual issue terms, including maturity, coupon, redemption provisions, security, and relevant disclosures. A rating is one input into credit analysis; it does not establish that a person owns the issue. [B03]

### Match the bond to the right verification route

The following are recommended investigation routes, not guarantees of a successful recovery.

| Bond category | Recommended starting evidence | Question to resolve early |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. Treasury marketable security | TreasuryDirect holding record or regulated intermediary's custody record; issue identifier and maturity | Is this the account holder's current position, and can it be transferred? |
| U.S. savings bond | Registration, series, issue date, serial number, and applicable Treasury ownership or redemption process | Does the requester have the right to redeem or obtain a reissue? |
| Corporate bond | Offering terms, trustee or paying-agent information, issuer disclosures, and custodian statement | Is the issue outstanding, paid, called, restructured, or in default? |
| Municipal bond | Official statement, continuing disclosures, issue details, and custody records | Which legal issuer owes payment, and from what revenue or security? |
| Foreign sovereign or corporate bond | Original terms, issuing jurisdiction, paying agent, currency, and subsequent notices | Are there exchange, restructuring, transfer, or enforcement issues? |
| Historical paper certificate | Original document, provenance, issuer history, cancellation markings, and successor records | Does it retain an enforceable financial claim or only possible collectible value? |

For U.S. municipal securities, the MSRB's EMMA system is a primary starting point for official statements and continuing disclosures. A municipal project name may differ from the legal issuer, so search the issue and issuer carefully. [B06]

### Build a bond identification record

Record the issuer's exact legal name, country, instrument type, identifier, currency, original face amount, amount currently held, coupon, issue date, maturity, and any call or conversion terms. Copy these fields from the complete document and compare them against independent records. Note inconsistencies explicitly: a matching issuer name cannot cure a different maturity or currency.

An identifier such as a CUSIP or ISIN is useful for matching records. Treat a match as identification evidence. It does not, by itself, establish current ownership, authenticity of an uploaded certificate, or freedom from liens. A copied number can appear on an unrelated or altered document.

For a paper certificate, preserve clear images of both sides, endorsements, attached coupons, stamps, signatures, cancellation punches, and amendments. Do not clean, mark, detach coupons, or surrender the original while deciding how it should be reviewed. Record who holds it and how it was obtained.

For an electronic holding, request an institution-generated statement through a channel the holder controls. Obtain authorization for the custodian to confirm the relevant position directly to the reviewing professional. Screenshots can help locate information, but should remain supporting material until independently confirmed.

### Verify three different relationships

**The issuer-to-instrument relationship:** Ask the issuer, registrar, trustee, or appropriate agent whether the described issue exists and its present status. Locate that organization independently. An issuer's ordinary customer-service team may need to refer the question to investor relations, treasury, or the paying agent.

**The holder-to-instrument relationship:** Establish who appears in the relevant ownership or custody records. A certificate registered to a deceased relative, dissolved business, or unrelated person requires an explanation supported by legally effective documents. Possession alone may not resolve entitlement.

**The holder-to-transaction relationship:** Determine whether that holder can sell, transfer, pledge, or redeem now. Obtain the required consents and documents for the specific action. Do not use a satisfactory answer about ownership as a substitute for transfer approval.

Ask whether the position is subject to a pledge, account restriction, court order, unsettled transaction, replacement-certificate process, or adverse claim. The appropriate search and confirmation methods depend on how the security is held and the governing law. Record the scope of each check instead of labeling the asset universally lien-free.

### Establish a usable value

Keep separate fields for face value, accrued amounts where applicable, observed market information, a dealer's actual quote, and expected proceeds after stated costs. Ask the quoting institution what quantity, settlement date, currency, and conditions its quote covers. If a quoted price excludes accrued interest, request the total settlement calculation.

FINRA's TRACE provides transaction information for covered fixed-income securities. EMMA publishes municipal trade price and yield information. These are useful market references; their records do not identify an uploaded document's owner or provide an executable offer for that person's position. [B04][B16]

For an inactive issue, state that no usable current bid has been obtained. Avoid replacing a missing market with an assumed percentage of face value. If the proposal involves lending, ask the lender to state its valuation method and eligible collateral requirements in writing.

TreasuryDirect explains that a marketable Treasury security held there must be transferred to a bank, broker, or dealer before it can be sold. Its initial holding-period rules also affect timing. This illustrates why a real, valuable holding still needs an executable transaction path. [B05]

### Historical and foreign paper

Treasury warns that some historic private bonds were once genuine obligations but no longer have security value. Wording referencing the United States can indicate where a private issuer operated, rather than a federal guarantee. Treasury also rejects claims that its sinking funds or approved high-yield programs will redeem such paper. [B01]

For an old foreign bond, ask a qualified specialist to trace the issuer, governing terms, payment history, successor arrangements, restructuring offers, and any applicable limitation periods. The age or appearance of the certificate does not settle those questions. Separate a collectible appraisal from a legal opinion about an enforceable debt claim, and separate both from evidence of an actual buyer.

Treasury's known-phony guidance describes purported 1934 Federal Reserve instruments accompanied by elaborate boxes and certificates. Its denomination warning concerns $100 million and $500 million Treasury bonds during the historical period alleged in that fraud. It is not a statement that every modern instrument bearing a large amount is fraudulent. [B02]

Offers involving secret bank trading, guaranteed extraordinary returns, or humanitarian programs supposedly backed by historic paper should trigger specific verification of the asserted issuer, program, and payment obligation. Treasury's historical-bond warning addresses those program claims. Do not pay a fee merely because a promoter supplies an impressive narrative. [B01]

## 3. SKRs: verify the custody relationship and the property

### What the label does and does not establish

In a review, use “SKR” as a label requiring clarification. Ask whether the document is a securities custody receipt, a warehouse receipt, an acknowledgment of a sealed package, or a different contractual record. Its heading cannot answer what was accepted, whether the contents were inspected, or who can obtain delivery.

OCC guidance provides a concrete regulated example: banks safekeeping U.S. government securities for customers must issue required confirmations or receipts and maintain records identifying the securities and customer interests. The handbook discusses reconciliation and safekeeping controls. These requirements concern that particular activity; they do not transform every privately issued document called an SKR into a government-backed asset. [B07]

An authentic receipt and verified underlying property are separate findings. If a custodian confirms receiving a sealed box, write exactly that. Do not elevate the finding to verified bullion, gemstones, negotiable securities, or an agreed value unless the relevant examination and confirmation support it.

### Build the custody evidence package

Request the complete receipt, custody agreement, current account statement, inventory schedule, amendments, storage invoices, relevant insurance information, and documents authorizing the depositor to place the property in custody. Where appropriate, obtain purchase records, title documents, transport records, independent inspection reports, and the chain of custody.

Use a line-by-line reconciliation:

| Field | Review question |
|---|---|
| Issuing entity | Which legal entity issued the receipt, and what authority or role does it claim? |
| Customer and depositor | Are these the same person, and who is entitled to give instructions? |
| Property description | Is it specific enough to distinguish this property from another person's property? |
| Quantity and identifiers | Do weights, serial numbers, lot numbers, and document schedules agree? |
| Storage location | Can an authorized reviewer confirm the relevant holding through the custodian? |
| Inspection scope | Was the property examined, sampled, weighed, assayed, or merely received sealed? |
| Release provisions | What must happen before property can be delivered or transferred? |
| Charges and rights | What fees, liens, pledges, adverse claims, or other restrictions are recorded? |

For metals or gems, a description copied from the depositor's invoice does not replace examination of the goods. An assay report should be matched to the actual lot and sampling method. An insurance document should be examined for the insured party, covered property, limits, exclusions, and policy period. Insurance evidence does not establish ownership or automatically establish recoverable value.

### Ask the custodian directly

With appropriate authorization, have a reviewer contact the custodian using independently obtained details. Confirm whether the receipt number is valid, whether it remains current, what property is recorded, who controls release, and whether amendments or replacement receipts exist. Ask for a dated response specifying the records checked and the limits of the answer.

If confidentiality prevents disclosure, arrange an authorized verification process. A refusal to discuss another customer's account does not prove fraud. An unresolved refusal does mean the reviewer cannot yet claim the holding has been independently established.

Clarify whether the contract identifies specific property reserved for the customer or describes a quantity-based claim against the provider. Examine any authority to pool, substitute, lend, or otherwise use the property. Treat marketing terms such as allocated and segregated as questions to confirm against the agreement and inventory records.

### Warehouse rights, liens, and delivery

Legal effects depend on the receipt and governing law. For example, Florida's warehouse-receipt provisions specify required information and address a warehouse's lien for certain storage and related charges. They also provide rules for enforcing that lien. These are reasons to investigate charges and release conditions even when the warehouse and receipt are genuine. A label alone does not determine negotiability or title. [B08]

For the proposed transaction, request a current schedule of charges, disclosure of recorded claims, and written delivery conditions. Where a lender will rely on the property, have its counsel determine what acknowledgment, control arrangement, security agreement, filing, or other step is needed. Do not assume that handing over the receipt creates every right the lender requires.

### A practical release check

Ask the proposed recipient or financing institution to identify the exact custody arrangement it accepts. Then reconcile its requirements with the custodian's actual procedures. A supervised inspection, authenticated custodian acknowledgment, or agreed transfer procedure may resolve the next question; which step is appropriate depends on the asset and transaction.

Do not describe an asset as monetizable merely because a third party advertises an SKR conversion service. Ask who supplies the money, what property or claim it acquires, when ownership or control changes, and which conditions could prevent payment.

## 4. SBLCs: read the undertaking, then verify the bank

### The essential relationship

An SBLC is a bank undertaking supporting obligations of its customer, often involving payment if the customer fails to meet a financial or performance obligation. OCC guidance treats the standby as an independent undertaking and discusses the bank's exposure when it honors and seeks reimbursement. A standby's stated amount is therefore not equivalent to unrestricted cash deposited for its beneficiary. [B09]

Identify the applicant, issuing bank, beneficiary, any advising bank, and any confirming bank. Record each role from the operative documents. The institution relaying an instrument and an institution undertaking to honor it may have different responsibilities; verify each claimed obligation explicitly.

For review purposes, the central question is: **Can this named beneficiary make the required presentation, to the specified place, within the applicable time, under an authenticated and effective undertaking?**

### Read the complete operative terms

Obtain the full issuance and every amendment. Extract:

- Issuer, issuing office, beneficiary, applicant, and reference number.
- Maximum amount, currency, reductions, reinstatements, and remaining availability.
- Effective date, expiry, presentation location, and permitted presentation method.
- Required demand, certificates, supporting documents, signatures, and exact statements.
- Partial or multiple drawing provisions, transfer language, and assignment provisions.
- Incorporated rules, governing law, jurisdiction, and relevant bank charges.
- Extension, nonextension, cancellation, and amendment procedures.

Treat this as a working checklist, not a substitute for reading the instrument. Ask the beneficiary's bank or trade-finance adviser to test a hypothetical presentation against the terms. A beneficiary may be unable to provide a required third-party certificate or meet a short deadline even where the standby is genuine.

### ISP98, UCP 600, and URDG 758

| Rules | General orientation | Review implication |
|---|---|---|
| ISP98 | Rules developed specifically for standby letters of credit | Check standby-specific provisions and any express modifications. |
| UCP 600 | Documentary-credit rules that can also apply to standbys when incorporated, to the extent applicable | Check which documentary-credit provisions apply to the particular standby. |
| URDG 758 | Rules for demand guarantees and counter-guarantees | Determine whether the undertaking is a demand guarantee and whether these rules are incorporated. |

ICC explains differences between UCP 600 and ISP98, including examination and expiry-related treatment. Its rules catalog identifies URDG 758 as the demand-guarantee rules. These regimes are not interchangeable merely because the documents perform similar commercial functions. [B10][B14]

Record the rules exactly as incorporated. Do not guess presentation deadlines or default expiry effects from a summary. The actual text, its amendments, applicable mandatory law, and current professional interpretation must resolve a transaction-specific question.

### MT760 and MT799: what the message establishes

SWIFT's cited Category 7 guide identifies MT760 as a message for issuing a demand guarantee or standby letter of credit and MT799 as a free-format message. Crucially, it states that, unless otherwise specified, an independent undertaking issued or advised through SWIFT constitutes an operative instrument. An MT760 can therefore carry the operative undertaking; describing every SWIFT message as merely informational would be wrong. [B11]

A screenshot labeled MT760 does not show that the recipient bank authenticated the message or accepted the proposed transaction. An MT799 label alone does not prove blocked funds, cash collateral, payment, or a funding commitment. Have the receiving bank confirm the authenticated communication and explain exactly what its content commits the relevant institution to do.

The cited SWIFT guide is dated 2022. Use it for those distinctions; the participating banks must apply the format and rules relevant to their actual transaction.

### Verify the institution and undertaking separately

Identify the bank's exact legal entity, branch, regulator, and operational contact through independent channels. A genuine bank name on a document does not establish issuance. Request authorized bank-to-bank authentication through the beneficiary's bank or other appropriate professional route.

Ask the receiving bank to confirm the reference, parties, amount, issuance status, amendments, expiry, and any apparent limitations. Ask whether the proposed financing institution has reviewed and accepted the final wording. Evidence that a bank received a message does not answer whether it will lend against it.

Keep a dated record of the confirming institution, the scope of its response, and any unresolved terms. Do not describe a bank employee's general acknowledgment as a legal opinion, credit approval, or undertaking to fund.

### Transfer of drawing rights versus assignment of proceeds

These are separate transaction questions. Florida provides one statutory example: section 675.112 generally conditions transfer of a beneficiary's drawing rights on the credit being transferable, subject to specified exceptions and conditions. Its text also addresses an issuer's duties regarding a requested transfer. Do not apply that provision as universal cross-border law. [B12]

Florida section 675.114 distinguishes proceeds from the right to draw. It allows assignment of proceeds but generally does not require an issuer or nominated person to recognize the assignment until it consents. The provision also addresses competing rights. An assignment does not itself make the assignee the beneficiary entitled to present a demand. [B13]

For any financing proposal, ask who must make the demand, who receives honored proceeds, whose consent is required, and what happens if no compliant demand occurs. Have counsel and the participating bank confirm the result under the actual instrument and governing law.

## 5. Authenticity does not establish financeability

Asset Machine should report evidence about the asset separately from a buyer's or lender's decision. The following proposed workflow keeps that distinction visible:

1. Identify the asset, obligation, or custody claim precisely.
2. Confirm its current status through an appropriate independent party.
3. Establish the requester's legal authority for the intended transaction.
4. Resolve material restrictions, competing claims, and delivery requirements.
5. Obtain current valuation evidence appropriate to the intended action.
6. Obtain the actual recipient's written acceptance and conditions.
7. Agree settlement procedures and complete the required documents.

Avoid invented advance rates, automatic monetization promises, and assumptions that a “top bank” name makes any wording acceptable. Ask what the money provider can enforce, what it can receive, and what remains uncertain. If its acceptance is missing, the financing outcome remains unconfirmed.

## 6. Hypothetical review cases

**Inherited corporate certificate.** A family produces a clean certificate with intact coupons. The reviewer identifies the issue and contacts the appropriate registrar or successor. Until outstanding status and estate authority are established, the finding is “historical document submitted; financial entitlement unresolved.” Its appearance does not establish a payable balance.

**Genuine receipt for sealed containers.** A warehouse confirms issuing a receipt and holding the containers. It has not inspected their contents. The reviewer records the custody confirmation and separately requests an authorized inspection and identification process. The result cannot yet say that the claimed gold exists in the stated quantity or purity.

**Verified bullion with unpaid storage.** Inventory and ownership records reconcile, but release requires payment of documented charges and resolution of a recorded pledge. The review can report the evidence supporting existence while keeping unrestricted delivery unresolved. A proposed buyer needs an agreed release and settlement process.

**Authenticated standby with difficult drawing terms.** The bank authenticates the instrument, but a required certificate depends on an outside party. The review records authentication and the presentation obstacle separately. The beneficiary's adviser must determine whether the requirement can be satisfied within the permitted period.

**MT799 offered as a funding commitment.** The message is authenticated, but its wording only describes a willingness to discuss a transaction. The reviewer reports what it says and asks for the actual financing agreement. Authentication does not add a promise absent from the message.

**Large historical “Federal Reserve bond.”** A document resembles a category in Treasury's warnings. The reviewer compares the exact claimed issuer, date, and form with official guidance and routes the case for appropriate verification. The review should identify the specific inconsistency rather than claiming all large-denomination financial documents are false.

## 7. Questions to send to the relevant professional

**For an issuer, registrar, or securities custodian:** Does this exact issue and holding exist in your records? What is its present status? Who is entitled to act? What restrictions or outstanding steps affect the requested sale, transfer, pledge, or redemption? What information can you confirm directly under the holder's authorization?

**For a warehouse or other custodian:** What property did you receive, what did you inspect, and what do you currently hold? Which agreement governs it? Who controls release? What charges or recorded claims apply? What procedure permits independent verification and delivery?

**For the beneficiary's bank or trade-finance adviser:** Is the undertaking authenticated and operative? What amendments apply? Can the beneficiary satisfy the presentation terms? Which rules and law govern? Is transfer or an assignment proposed, and what consent is needed? Has the intended financier accepted the final terms?

**For the proposed buyer or lender:** What exactly are you acquiring or accepting as collateral? What value and conditions are you using? What evidence remains outstanding? Who bears each fee? What must occur before funds are released?

The resulting review should state each supported finding, its source and date, its limits, and the next unresolved question. That is a stronger basis for a transaction than a single unexplained label such as “real,” “verified,” or “bankable.”

# Inherited Documents, Gold, and Gemstones

Research date: November 3, 2026. These chapters describe proposed Asset Machine review standards and educational workflows. They do not establish that Asset Machine has examined an asset or implemented a verification service. Legal examples identify their jurisdiction; actual ownership, estate authority, taxation, and transfer requirements require the applicable law and asset documents.

## I1. What inherited documents can establish

An inherited folder may contain valuable evidence without containing a presently transferable asset. Review six separate questions: does the asset exist, who owns it, who controls it, what is it worth, can it be transferred, and would a lender accept it? A positive answer to one question does not answer the others.

A will expresses testamentary instructions. Probate may establish its legal effect and authorize a representative to administer relevant property. Other assets may pass through a trust, survivorship, or beneficiary arrangement. California's court guidance illustrates these different routes; other jurisdictions have their own procedures. A copy of a will usually does not by itself satisfy a bank's or purchaser's transfer requirements. [I01]

| Evidence | What it helps establish | What remains to be checked |
|---|---|---|
| Certified death record | A recorded death and the person's identifying details | Ownership, beneficiary rights, estate authority |
| Will or trust instrument | Instructions, named beneficiaries, or fiduciary provisions | Validity, amendments, applicable law, assets actually covered |
| Court appointment and current letters | Appointment and stated powers of an estate representative | Restrictions, revocation, transaction-specific orders |
| Current custodian or registry record | Registered account or property information | Authority to transfer, competing claims, relevant restrictions |
| Appraisal or valuation report | An opinion for a stated purpose and date | Achievable sale proceeds, costs, title, liquidity |
| Completed transfer record | Evidence of a particular registration or delivery | Subsequent transfers, liens, and present status |

This is an evidence map, not a universal list of legally sufficient documents. For each item, record its issuer, date, reference number, source of verification, and the exact proposition it supports.

## I2. Establish the representative's authority

Start with the deceased person's legal name, relevant name variations, date of death, domicile, and the location and registration of each asset. These facts help the reviewing lawyer identify which estate process applies. Do not assume one court appointment resolves assets in every country.

An executor named in a will and a person currently authorized to administer an estate are not automatically the same proposition. California court terminology distinguishes the proposed executor from the court-appointed personal representative; administrators may be appointed in other circumstances. [I02]

Obtain the appointment documents from the relevant court or a verified official channel. Read the powers and limitations. California's DE-150 form, for example, distinguishes full and limited authority and identifies transactions requiring court supervision under limited authority. A recognizable court seal does not eliminate those restrictions. [I03]

**Asset Machine recommended review:** identify the proceeding and court; match the representative's identity; obtain a sufficiently current record of appointment; check relevant later orders; and have the receiving institution confirm its required transfer package. If the representative is selling to themselves, has a conflict, or faces a beneficiary objection, obtain specific legal review before treating the proposed transaction as authorized.

An apostille authenticates specified features of a public document's origin, such as a signature, official capacity, or seal. It does not establish the truth of the document's contents. Therefore, an apostilled inheritance declaration is not independent proof that the listed bullion exists or belongs to the declarant. [I04]

## I3. Trusts, beneficiaries, and international estates

Separate assets by the route through which they are claimed. For trust property, request the relevant trust documentation, evidence identifying the current trustee, and records connecting the asset to that trust. For jointly registered or beneficiary-designated assets, obtain the actual registration and the provider's transfer requirements. Being named in a will is not a substitute for checking how a particular account was registered. These are proposed document-review steps, not a determination of legal entitlement.

In the United States, transfer-on-death arrangements for securities may permit transfer to registered beneficiaries outside probate, subject to state law and the institution's arrangements. The SEC advises contacting the broker or transfer agent about the necessary paperwork. [I05]

For cross-border estates, ask a qualified adviser which court decision or foreign grant is recognized, whether a local proceeding is required, and which certified translations or authentication formalities apply. Record these as unresolved until the relevant institution or professional confirms them. Do not advertise a single “international inheritance certificate” as sufficient for every asset and country.

Death records, tax numbers, identity documents, account details, and family relationships should be handled in a restricted review process. Public educational examples should use fictional details and redacted documents.

## I4. Inherited securities and savings bonds

A paper share certificate can be genuine while the associated company has merged, reorganized, cancelled the security, or changed its transfer arrangements. The practical objective is to connect the certificate to a current issuer or transfer-agent record and establish the claimant's entitlement. Ask for the security description, registration, certificate or account number, corporate-action history, and transfer requirements. Record unresolved links instead of treating the printed face amount as cash value.

Signature guarantees and notarization serve different purposes. When an institution requires a securities signature guarantee, ask it which guarantee it accepts; an ordinary notarization should not be assumed to satisfy that requirement. [I05]

U.S. savings bonds have their own Treasury procedures. The appropriate route depends on how the bond is registered, including any surviving co-owner or beneficiary, and whether an estate representative is involved. Use TreasuryDirect's current death-of-owner instructions for that registration. [I06] Treasury separately explains the rights and procedures for registered surviving co-owners and beneficiaries. [I14]

For a court-appointed representative, Treasury's instructions address the documentation needed to redeem or reissue eligible bonds. A family agreement or photocopied certificate should not be substituted for Treasury's required evidence. [I07]

**Recommended evidence packet:** the security or account details, verified issuer contact, current status confirmation, evidence of the deceased owner's registration, death documentation, evidence of the claimant's authority, required transfer forms, and the institution's completed transfer confirmation. Keep market valuation separate from this ownership review.

## I5. Land, deeds, and mineral interests

A deed describes a transaction; a present-day ownership review must also consider later records and relevant burdens. Obtain the current official record from the appropriate registry and ask a local property professional which searches are needed for the proposed transaction. An old deed in a family archive is a starting point.

In England and Wales, HM Land Registry provides information about registered property, including title documents. The service and evidential significance differ from those of a county recorder in the United States. [I08] HM Land Registry also distinguishes official copies from other ways of viewing or obtaining information; obtain the form required for the intended legal use. [I09]

Mineral ownership needs separate attention. Texas guidance explains that surface and mineral ownership can be separated; owning the surface does not necessarily mean owning the minerals beneath it. [I10] Texas General Land Office materials describe the importance of relevant deeds and mineral instruments. Do not generalize Texas rules to other jurisdictions. [I11]

For an inherited mineral claim, the recommended packet includes the legal description, chain of relevant instruments, fractional interest, leases and assignments, applicable royalties, recorded burdens, and recent operator statements where relevant. Ask a specialist to reconcile the paperwork. A royalty payment supports a payment history; it does not independently resolve every title issue or establish the value of reserves.

## I6. Tax basis, estate value, and sale value

Keep three valuation questions separate: value required for estate administration or tax reporting, the beneficiary's tax basis, and likely net proceeds from a sale. The dates, assumptions, and governing rules may differ.

For U.S. federal tax purposes, inherited property's basis is generally its fair market value at the owner's death, subject to alternate valuation and other exceptions. That may increase or decrease basis. The IRS also describes special rules and basis-consistency requirements that can apply. Avoid the blanket claim that every inheritance receives a tax-free “step-up.” [I12]

The IRS distinguishes property received by inheritance from income generated by the property and from income in respect of a decedent. An inherited payment right, retirement interest, or accrued income item may require different treatment from an ordinary inherited capital asset. Estate and beneficiary reporting responsibilities also differ. [I13]

Preserve the valuation date, valuation report, methodology, supporting market information, acquisition history if available, and relevant estate tax or basis statements. Have a qualified tax adviser resolve missing records before a material disposition. Asset Machine should label a value by purpose and date instead of presenting an estate appraisal as a current cash offer.

## I7. Preserve originals and verify independently

Create a dated inventory before sending anything away. Photograph or scan both sides, envelopes, attachments, endorsements, and identifying marks. Preserve originals without writing on, laminating, repairing, or removing seals. Use a documented handover whenever an original must leave secure storage. Confirm why it is required and how it will be returned.

Local rules may require an original will to be deposited with a court. San Diego Superior Court's guidance is one example of court-specific will and estate procedures. Check the relevant court's instructions rather than relying on a universal storage rule. [I15]

Locate institutions through independently obtained official contact information. A telephone number printed on a disputed document is not independent verification. Record who responded, their role, when they responded, and what they actually confirmed. “We issued this document” is narrower than “this asset remains available for this claimant to transfer.”

### Inheritance examples and frequently asked questions

**Example: a will names a beneficiary of gold.** The will and death record support an inheritance investigation. The recommended next steps are to establish authority, locate the gold, verify custody and inventory, resolve competing rights, and document distribution. Until then, classify the claim as pending.

**Example: a deed says the family owns land.** A current search identifies later transfers and a separate mineral reservation. The review should report the interest actually supported by current evidence, rather than repeat the old document's description.

**Does a notary confirm that the inherited asset exists?** Do not infer asset existence from notarization or authentication of signatures. Identify the particular notarial act and obtain separate asset evidence.

**Can an heir sell immediately?** That depends on the asset, transfer route, authority, and governing law. Obtain transaction-specific confirmation rather than a general assurance that “the estate is valid.”

**Who makes the legal determination?** A qualified lawyer, relevant court, registry, or receiving institution addresses the issues within its remit. Asset Machine can organize evidence and identify gaps without claiming universal authority to certify ownership.

## G1. Define the gold claim before reviewing it

First identify what is being offered: physical bars, coins, scrap, jewelry, an account balance, an interest in a pooled holding, a contractual delivery promise, or an interest in a mining business. These are different claims with different evidence requirements.

LBMA describes allocated holdings as specific bars identified for a customer, while an unallocated account represents a general entitlement to metal rather than ownership of a specified bar inventory. The precise rights and risks also depend on the account agreement and applicable law. [G01]

For a custody claim, request the agreement and ask: which legal entity holds the metal; in what capacity; how the customer's entitlement is recorded; what identification is available; whether substitution or lending is permitted; what withdrawal procedure applies; and what happens in an insolvency. These questions need answers from the actual contractual documents and appropriate advisers.

A warehouse receipt, Safe Keeping Receipt, or account screenshot should lead to verification with the named custodian. It should not replace it. Record the custodian's independently verified identity, the scope of its confirmation, and any limitation on access or withdrawal.

## G2. Build an inventory that can be reconciled

The recommended inventory records refiner or manufacturer, serial number where present, bar type, gross weight, stated fineness, fine-metal quantity, location, owner or account reference, and the dates of receipt and inspection. Reconcile this inventory against the custody records and the physical items. Small retail products may use packaging or identifiers different from wholesale bars.

LBMA's Good Delivery specifications set requirements for qualifying wholesale bars, including fineness and identifying marks. They are not a universal design standard for every legitimate gold product. [G02]

Document movements and seals, who had custody, and whether the inventory was inspected, sampled, or merely reproduced from records. When a report covers only selected items, display its sample scope. Ask how discrepancies, substitutions, and damaged packaging were resolved. Independent access matters: a reviewer who sees only seller-selected photographs cannot report the same finding as a reviewer who controls the examination and item selection.

Separately investigate rights over the metal. An assay addresses material composition; it does not establish ownership, freedom from liens, or an unrestricted right of withdrawal. An insurance document should be reviewed for the actual insured parties, location, coverage, exclusions, and period rather than treated as title evidence.

## G3. Test the metal with a method appropriate to the claim

Photos, hallmarks, packaging, and simple screening can identify inconsistencies. They cannot alone establish the composition of an entire bar. A magnet response is not a universal authenticity test, and a visually convincing surface is not proof of a homogeneous interior.

LBMA's Physical Market Guidelines explain the limits of nondestructive methods and the importance of representative material when assessing composition. Surface methods require care where homogeneity is uncertain. The test plan should therefore address the product's construction and the possibility of internal substitution, rather than merely confirm a plausible surface reading. [G03]

**Recommended professional testing brief:** identify the exact items; record weights and dimensions; choose complementary techniques appropriate to those items; state calibration and uncertainty; specify the sampling plan; identify destructive work requiring consent; and report results against the declared composition. X-ray fluorescence generally provides information about the tested surface region. A result from that region should not be described as proof of the composition of every part of a large item. [G03]

Avoid home acid, drilling, melting, or chemical experiments. Arrange testing with a competent independent laboratory or refiner, with an agreed chain of custody and written permission for any destructive procedure. The report should identify which items and samples it actually examined. A clean sample supplied by the seller does not establish the composition of an unexamined shipment.

## G4. Understand what industry standards establish

A refiner's appearance on an industry list may support a claim about that refiner's recognized status. It does not authenticate every object bearing its logo, verify every intermediary using its name, or establish a particular seller's title. LBMA's Good Delivery FAQs explain the purpose and scope of its accreditation framework. [G04]

Use the standard as one evidence component. Check the relevant list or specification directly, then connect the individual bar, actual custodian, claimant, and proposed transaction through their own evidence. Describe the result precisely: “manufacturer appears on the referenced list” is different from “this bar was examined and matched to this inventory.”

## G5. Calculate value with units and costs visible

A transparent metal-content calculation is:

**Fine-metal quantity = gross metal weight × fineness expressed as a decimal.**

**Illustrative metal-content value = fine-metal quantity × a stated price per matching unit.**

For example, assume a fictional bar with a gross metal weight of one troy ounce, fineness of 0.999, and a fictional reference price of $2,000 per fine troy ounce. The calculation is 1 × 0.999 × $2,000 = **$1,998** before costs. This is arithmetic using invented inputs, not a current quotation or an offer to buy.

Keep grams, kilograms, ordinary ounces, and troy ounces distinct. Record the price source, timestamp, currency, weight convention, and whether weight includes packaging or non-gold components. Explain any premium or discount. Then identify actual sale costs, such as testing, refining, transport, insurance, commissions, and applicable taxes. For collectible coins or jewelry, separate metal content from any independently supported collectible or workmanship value.

## G6. Underground gold is not a deliverable bullion inventory

Mining terminology has defined meanings within particular reporting systems. U.S. securities disclosure rules distinguish mineral resources from mineral reserves; reserve classification requires consideration of technical and economic factors under the relevant definitions. These categories concern deposits and mining prospects, not bars already available in a vault. [G05]

For an underground-gold proposal, request the applicable technical report, author and qualifications, effective date, mineral-rights evidence, reporting standard, assumptions, permits, and financing needs. These are recommended review inputs, not a finding that a project is viable. Have an appropriately qualified mining professional evaluate geology, recovery, extraction costs, and the resource or reserve classification.

Multiplying estimated gold in the ground by a bullion quotation does not account for recovery, development, operating costs, time, financing, or project risk. Do not label that arithmetic “cash value,” “proof of funds,” or available collateral without a separate, supported basis.

## G7. Identify gemstones accurately

Gemstone identity has several independent dimensions. The FTC's jewelry guidance addresses representations about natural, laboratory-created, imitation, and treated products, including relevant disclosures. [G06]

| Question | Distinction the review should preserve |
|---|---|
| What material is it? | Diamond, ruby, sapphire, another mineral, glass, or another substance |
| How did it form? | Natural or laboratory-created, where applicable |
| Is it a simulant? | A different material used to resemble the named gemstone |
| Has it been treated? | Treatment type, detectability, permanence, and care implications |
| What qualities matter? | Weight, dimensions, appearance, condition, and relevant grading |
| What provenance is supported? | Evidence for geographic origin or supply-chain history, with limits |

A laboratory-created diamond is diamond of a different origin; a diamond simulant is a different material. A natural stone may also be treated. Preserve these distinctions in listings, searches, educational examples, and valuation records. “Real” is too ambiguous to communicate them reliably. [G06]

GIA explains that colored-stone identification can involve trained examination and scientific instruments. Visual resemblance or a seller's handheld demonstration should not replace an appropriate laboratory examination for a valuable or uncertain stone. [G11]

## G8. Verify the report and match the actual stone

GIA Report Check allows users to retrieve information associated with a GIA report number. Enter the number through GIA's independently located service and compare the retrieved details with the supplied document. A successful lookup supports the existence of that report record. It does not, by itself, establish that the seller holds the stone described. [G07]

The recommended physical match considers dimensions, weight, shape, identifying features, report comments, and any inscription. Use a qualified examiner, especially when a setting limits observation. Record how the stone was identified before and after custody changes. GIA's inscription-matching guidance describes one verification method; do not assume a visible number resolves the entire identity question. [G15]

Counterfeit inscriptions are a documented problem. GIA has reported natural and laboratory-grown diamonds bearing inscriptions intended to imitate other reported stones. That makes independent physical matching essential when the transaction risk warrants it. [G10]

Read the report's scope. GIA explains that its colored-stone reports identify the material and address detectable treatments and, when available, geographic origin; they do not provide a general quality grade equivalent to diamond grading. [G09] “Origin undetermined” should remain undetermined. Do not convert a qualified laboratory opinion into a guaranteed mine location or complete history.

## G9. Separate identification, appraisal, and achievable sale value

A laboratory report identifies or grades within its stated scope. It does not establish legal ownership or promise a buyer. GIA explicitly states that it does not appraise diamonds. [G08]

For a separate appraisal, require the purpose, valuation date, market considered, currency, assumptions, credentials, independence, and description of the examined item. Ask whether the figure concerns replacement, retail sale, wholesale trade, liquidation, tax reporting, or another purpose. Comparing values with different purposes can create a false impression of profit.

For rough gemstones, the recommended assessment also considers identity, treatment uncertainty, fractures, inclusions, potential cutting yield, usable quality distribution, and processing costs. A parcel should have a defined sampling method, selection process, size, and stated limits. An attractive sample selected by the seller cannot establish that every stone has the same quality.

Do not value a rough parcel by multiplying total weight by the highest advertised price of a fine polished stone. Obtain appropriate independent expertise and, where sale value matters, credible market evidence or actual bids for the described goods. Report uncertainty as a range or unresolved issue rather than presenting an unsupported precise total.

## G10. Provenance and responsible sourcing

The Kimberley Process concerns the international trade in rough diamonds and uses a specific conflict-diamond definition connected to rebel movements financing conflict against legitimate governments. Its scope should not be stretched into a guarantee covering every gemstone, every polished diamond, or every social and environmental concern. [G12]

The Kimberley Process also distinguishes its certification arrangements from industry warranty practices. Review the exact document, shipment, issuer, and applicable scope instead of accepting an undifferentiated “conflict-free certificate.” [G13]

OECD guidance provides a risk-based due-diligence framework for mineral supply chains, including management systems, risk identification, responses, independent audit where relevant, and reporting. It is a framework for an ongoing process, not a universal ownership or ethical-quality certificate for an individual stone. [G14]

The recommended evidence packet preserves invoices, supplier identities, relevant export and import documents, lot references, custody records, applicable certificates, and documented risk-review findings. State gaps clearly. A laboratory opinion on geological origin does not document each commercial transfer or every condition under which the material was produced.

## G11. Worked cases and review outcomes

**Gold case:** a seller presents a genuine laboratory result and a photograph of sealed bars. The report identifies only a sample. The correct finding is that the sample was examined within the report's scope; the shipment's composition, ownership, and availability remain unresolved until additional evidence connects them.

**Gemstone case:** a report number resolves correctly, but independent examination finds different dimensions or identifying features. Stop treating the report as evidence for that stone. Preserve the discrepancy and obtain the laboratory's guidance before continuing the transaction.

**Valuation case:** an inherited ring has a replacement appraisal of $30,000 and an actual purchase offer of $8,000. Neither number automatically invalidates the other. Review purpose, date, assumptions, market, and the offered transaction terms before drawing a conclusion.

**Does one certificate prove everything?** No single document answers existence, ownership, custody, value, transferability, and financeability. Keep evidence and findings separate for each question.

**Can genuine gold or gemstones still be unsuitable collateral?** Yes. A lender must assess its own legal rights, custody arrangements, valuation, liquidity, and eligibility requirements. Material authenticity alone does not demonstrate lender acceptance.

**What should Asset Machine display?** Use specific findings such as “issuer record matched,” “physical examination completed for listed items,” “custody confirmation dated,” “title review pending,” and “valuation prepared for stated purpose.” Include reviewer, date, scope, and unresolved conditions. Avoid a blanket “verified real asset” badge that implies checks the evidence does not support.

# Wallets and Digital Asset Verification

Research date: November 3, 2026. The procedures below are proposed Asset Machine review standards. They describe the evidence a reviewer should collect; they do not certify any submitted asset or establish that the platform already performs these checks. Legal conclusions depend on the actual transaction, parties, governing law, and current rules.

## W1 What a wallet actually proves

A wallet is an interface and authorization arrangement for interacting with digital assets. An address identifies an account or spending condition on a particular network. A public address can be viewed without controlling it. Ethereum distinguishes key-controlled accounts from accounts governed by smart-contract logic; wallet software is separate from the account itself. [W01]

Keep four claims separate:

| Claim | Relevant evidence | What remains unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A balance exists | Independently obtained chain state at a recorded block | Identity, title, access, value |
| A claimant can authorize an action | A properly verified, limited challenge or applicable custody confirmation | Lawful ownership and authority to sell or pledge |
| The claimant owns an economic interest | Purchase, transfer, estate, trust, company, or custody records examined under applicable law | Whether restrictions or competing claims prevent the intended transaction |
| The asset can produce cash | A viable sale or redemption path with actual terms | Settlement, fees, price changes, counterparty performance |

An employee might control an employer's wallet. A trustee might operate a trust wallet. A thief might possess a stolen key. A customer might own a contractual interest held through an exchange without controlling the exchange's blockchain address. These examples show why technical access and legal ownership require different evidence.

For watch-only claims, request independent proof of the relevant authorization rather than a screen recording. Bitcoin Core's documentation even distinguishes knowing how an output could be spent from possessing the necessary private keys. A software label therefore cannot substitute for a verifiable demonstration. [W02]

## W2 Identify the exact asset before assessing it

The minimum digital-asset identifier is the network plus its network identifier and the asset's authoritative address or identifier. For a native coin, identify the network and native asset. For a fungible contract token, add its contract address. For an NFT, add both contract address and token ID. A ticker, logo, collection name, or wallet display name is insufficient.

ERC-20 defines balance and transfer functions and optional display fields, including token name, symbol, and decimals. Those display fields are not a registry of exclusive brand names or a guarantee of value. ERC-721 tracks distinct token identifiers and allows metadata references; a picture or metadata description is not independent verification of a physical asset. [W06][W11]

**Asset Machine intake fields**

- Claimed owner and capacity: individual, company officer, executor, trustee, custodian, or agent.
- Network name and identifier; mainnet or test environment.
- Native coin, token, NFT, wrapped asset, staked position, or custodial account claim.
- Exact public address, token contract, and token ID where applicable.
- Claimed quantity, units, source of acquisition, intended use, and acquisition evidence.
- Whether assets are directly held, pooled, locked, borrowed, pledged, bridged, or managed for others.
- Who independently confirms issuance, custody, redemption, or ownership records.

Resolve addresses from the issuer's independently located official materials, then compare all characters. Record disagreements rather than choosing the version with the largest displayed balance. Do not ask for a seed phrase, private key, wallet backup, or password.

## W3 Verify control without taking custody

For a supported Ethereum account, use a narrowly scoped sign-in challenge following ERC-4361. Bind the message to the actual application domain and URI, selected chain, address, a fresh unpredictable nonce, issue time, and a short expiration. Verify the parsed fields and signature server-side, consume the nonce once, and reject expired or mismatched requests. The resulting observation concerns that challenge at that time. [W03]

Contract wallets require their appropriate validation mechanism. ERC-1271 provides a standard way for a contract to report whether a signature is valid. Its result may depend on current contract state; a signature from one individual involved in a multisignature arrangement is not automatically authority for the whole wallet. Preserve the chain and block context used for verification. [W04]

For other chains or custody models, use a reviewed compatible process. Mark unsupported signing as **control not yet established**, not as proof of fraud. A regulated or institutional custodian may instead confirm an account relationship and authorized instructions through its established process. A customer should not be asked to obtain the custodian's private keys.

**Signature safety matters.** Some signed messages can grant spending rights. ERC-2612 explicitly permits token allowances to be changed using a signature. Consequently, a request described as verification must never silently become a permit, order, token approval, delegation, or transfer. Review the entire human-readable message and actual signing method; an absence of gas charges does not establish harmlessness. [W05]

Asset Machine should use a dedicated confirmation screen that states the purpose, domain, address, expiry, and that no asset movement or spending permission is requested. Reject opaque signing prompts and requests to install remote-access software. Do not require a deposit to prove wealth. If the workflow changes from evidence collection to a real transaction, it needs a separate instruction and transaction review.

## W4 Verify balances and transaction state independently

Record the chain, block height and hash, observation time, raw amount, display units, data source, and finality status. For a significant claim, reconcile two independent infrastructure sources or a properly operated node and an independent explorer. Two websites copying the same upstream feed should not be described as independent confirmations.

Check both transaction execution and resulting state. A visible transaction hash is not itself proof that the intended asset reached the intended account. Examine success or failure, exact recipient, relevant contract, and net balance change. A transaction may concern a similarly named token, a different chain, a zero amount, or a test environment.

Finality is network-specific. Ethereum's proof-of-stake finality uses checkpoint agreement, while proof-of-work systems have probabilistic settlement confidence. Do not impose a universal confirmation count across unrelated networks. Specify the network's actual mechanism and the review policy used. [W08]

**Recommended workflow:** save an initial observation, verify any submitted transfer against the same asset identifier, check whether the asset remains present at the review point, and refresh before a transaction relies on the result. Treat outages, conflicting chain states, and unavailable historical data as unresolved. A balance that existed last month cannot support an assertion about today's unencumbered holdings.

For bridged or wrapped assets, investigate the connection to the other system: where the backing is held, who can release it, and what happens if the bridge fails. Record each layer separately rather than presenting a wrapped balance as direct ownership of the underlying asset.

## W5 Review token controls and restrictions

A token can exist and still have severe transfer or redemption restrictions. The review should ask who can mint additional units, burn units, freeze addresses, change fees, restrict recipients, pause transfers, update logic, recover accounts, and change the controlling administrators. Identify multisignature thresholds, delegates, timelocks, and emergency powers where they exist.

Upgradeable contracts require special attention. ERC-1967 describes standardized storage locations for certain proxy implementations and administrators. Reviewing only a proxy address or an old audit report can miss the logic currently in use. Record implementation addresses and versions as of the observation block; investigate nonstandard upgrade mechanisms separately. [W07]

An explorer's source-code verification checks the correspondence between source and deployed bytecode; it does not establish that the behavior is safe. [W12] Match the deployed implementation to the reviewed source, then check the review's scope, date, exclusions, unresolved findings, and changes since the review. No document should be described as covering code it did not examine.

Asset Machine's proposed reviewer checklist should include:

- Can this holder transfer to the intended recipient under current rules?
- Does the holder need approval, identity clearance, or issuer consent?
- Are units locked, staked, escrowed, vested, or subject to a pending redemption?
- Is the balance a direct asset or a claim on another protocol or intermediary?
- Who can change these answers, and how quickly?
- What evidence supports any claim that the contract is immutable?

A simulation can expose current transaction problems, but it is not a promise that a later real transaction will succeed. Avoid executing trades or approvals merely to test eligibility. Unresolved controls should appear beside the balance, not hidden in a generic risk score.

## W6 Assess economic value and custody claims

For a marketable digital asset, record the actual market or redemption route, trading pair, observed time, quote currency, spread, executable depth, withdrawal restrictions, fees, and the quantity that could reasonably be sold. A small trade at a high price does not demonstrate that an entire holding can be sold at that price. A displayed dollar value should be labeled as an estimate with its calculation method.

For a custodial account, request a statement obtained through the institution's authenticated channel and confirm the named customer, relevant assets, permitted withdrawals, account restrictions, and any lending or pledge arrangements. A screenshot forwarded by a broker cannot independently establish those facts. Confirm who would owe delivery if the custodian, subcustodian, or issuer failed.

For stablecoins and asset-backed tokens, evaluate the holder's redemption rights: eligible redeemers, minimums, timing, fees, geographic limitations, suspension clauses, and the entity with the obligation. Do not assume that a token's target price creates an unconditional right for every holder to receive cash.

These questions are the platform's recommended evidence requirements, not a universal appraisal method. Obtain specialist valuation or legal review when the intended transaction depends on a defensible price or enforceable custody right. Record market value, contractual redemption amount, and proposed lender value separately; a lender makes its own credit decision.

## W7 Understand proof of reserves

The PCAOB's investor advisory explains that proof-of-reserves reports can omit liabilities, ownership rights, borrowed reserves, and events after the observation time. They are not equivalent to financial-statement audits. Even a report prepared by a registered accounting firm does not automatically receive the protections associated with a PCAOB-standard audit. [W10]

Asset Machine should capture the engagement type, assurance level if any, provider, date, assets covered, procedures performed, exclusions, and the precise assertion examined. Ask whether the evidence addresses obligations owed to all customers, encumbrances, asset availability, and the relationship between the report and the actual issuer or custodian.

A cryptographic inclusion proof may help a customer check that a particular account entry was included in a disclosed data set. It does not independently establish that all liabilities were included, that the entry is legally accurate, or that the corresponding reserves remain available. Evaluate the mathematical check and the underlying accounting claim separately. Do not convert a reserve ratio or inclusion check into a general solvency certificate.

## W8 Wallet review examples and questions

**Example 1 — A very large wallet screenshot.** A claimant provides an image showing millions of dollars. Review starts with the exact network and public address. If independent data shows a balance but the claimant cannot complete an appropriate challenge or provide a custody confirmation, report **balance observed; claimant control unconfirmed**. The absence of control evidence does not justify accusing the claimant of theft or fraud.

**Example 2 — A convincing token name.** A wallet displays a familiar stablecoin name, but its contract does not match the issuer's documented contract for that network. Record the mismatch and stop the valuation from inheriting the genuine asset's price. Determine what the received token actually is before continuing.

**Example 3 — A company treasurer signs successfully.** The challenge succeeds, but the documents identify a company as the owner. Request corporate authority and the scope of the treasurer's power. A successful signature does not authorize a personal sale of corporate property.

**Example 4 — A deposit is requested to activate value.** Someone asks the holder to pay a special fee to release a token's supposed market value. Independently check the contractual and network explanation. Ordinary network fees do not establish that a claimed investment or recovery scheme is legitimate.

Useful questions are: Who is the legal owner? Who holds signing authority? Is access shared? Why was the asset received? What prevents transfer? What is the evidence of acquisition? Is the value a real executable quote or a wallet estimate? Has any other party financed, pledged, or claimed the position?

# Tokenization and Evidence of Real Asset Backing

## T1 Define the holder's actual right

Tokenization creates a digital representation or record associated with an asset or legal relationship. The decisive question is: **What exactly can the holder enforce, against whom, and under which law?** The answer might be direct ownership, a beneficial interest, a security entitlement, a debt claim, a fund or company interest, a redemption right, or price exposure without ownership of the referenced asset.

The SEC's January 28, 2026 staff statement distinguishes issuer-sponsored securities from third-party arrangements, including custodial and synthetic structures. These structures can convey different rights and different exposure to intermediary insolvency. It is a staff statement, not a Commission rule or approval of a particular token. [T01]

Require a plain-language rights statement and the documents that support it. If the issuer says one token represents one gram of gold, ask whether that means title to identified metal, a pooled contractual entitlement, a claim against a company, or merely a price-linked promise. Each demands different evidence and a different explanation to the customer.

An NFT label, token standard, transaction record, or issuer's website cannot answer the legal-rights question by itself. Do not imply that a digital image of an inherited certificate creates a redeemable asset, that minting discharges an existing lien, or that a contract balance creates government recognition of the underlying document.

## T2 Map the parties and responsibilities

Prepare a responsibility map before approving any tokenization proposal:

| Role | Evidence to request | Question the reviewer must resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asset owner or originator | Title, acquisition, authority, restrictions | Can this party contribute the stated right? |
| Issuer or special-purpose entity | Formation documents, governing agreements, obligations | What does the token holder own or have a claim against? |
| Custodian or trustee | Executed custody terms and direct confirmations | What is held, for whom, and subject to whose claims? |
| Registry or transfer function | Authoritative ownership records and reconciliation process | Which record controls if systems disagree? |
| Servicer | Collection, reporting, default, and distribution duties | Who performs obligations over the asset's life? |
| Oracle or evidence publisher | Data sources, authority, update rules | Who asserts the facts used by the contract? |
| Contract administrator | Deployed code, keys, upgrade and emergency powers | Who can change balances, rules, or access? |
| Verifier or assurance provider | Engagement terms, scope, independence | Which statements were actually examined? |

These roles may be performed by fewer entities, but concentration and conflicts still need disclosure. A special-purpose entity is not automatically bankruptcy-proof. Ask counsel to analyze separateness, title, competing claims, and what holders could recover in a failure.

BIS and CPMI identify legal, settlement, credit, liquidity, governance, custody, and operational risks in token arrangements. They also distinguish technical processing from legally final settlement. Treat technology and the legal operating arrangement as a single review subject. [T08]

## T3 The proposed tokenization approval sequence

**Step 1 — Describe the intended transaction.** State the asset, current owner, jurisdictions, target holders, purpose, transaction size, and whether the platform is documenting, issuing, selling, brokering, safeguarding, or financing. The same asset can generate different obligations in different business models. Do not begin with a blockchain selection.

**Step 2 — Finish the underlying asset review.** Use the relevant bond, inheritance, gold, gem, property, or custody module. Preserve negative findings and unresolved questions. A tokenization proposal must not turn incomplete evidence into a positive asset-status badge.

**Step 3 — Establish the legal connection.** Obtain executed documents identifying the asset or pool, right granted, persons entitled, transfer mechanism, applicable law, dispute forum, restrictions, and priority. For a document that must be registered or recorded elsewhere, establish how that process is completed and how its outcome is reconciled with the digital record.

**Step 4 — Verify the actual backing arrangement.** Confirm asset location, custodian acceptance, authority, coverage, and any existing security interests. Reconcile the precise assets against the issuance plan. A business agreement saying assets will be deposited later is different from evidence that they are already held.

**Step 5 — Define the token terms.** Document units, supply, minting authority, eligible holders, transfer restrictions, distributions, fees, redemption, defaults, complaints, recovery, and termination. Explain whether holders can request physical delivery, cash, another security, or nothing before a specified event.

**Step 6 — Review implementation.** Match code behavior and administrative powers to the legal terms. Test ordinary transfers, ineligible recipients, over-issuance, partial redemption, paused operation, lost-key recovery, conflicting records, and backing shortfalls. A technical audit and legal review answer different questions; obtain each where needed.

**Step 7 — Approve a limited, documented issuance.** Require the designated human decision makers to approve a specific version of the asset package, terms, code, disclosures, and issuance quantity. Record reservations and expiry. A preparatory approval does not authorize unlimited future minting.

**Step 8 — Reconcile and monitor.** Record actual minted units, outstanding claims, custodial holdings, redemptions, and exceptions. Refresh evidence when assets move, terms change, a signer changes, an inspection expires, or a claim is disputed. Report the scope and date of each review publicly without exposing private source documents.

This sequence is Asset Machine's proposed operational standard, not a statement that every jurisdiction requires identical steps. An unresolved essential condition should prevent a positive readiness finding for the affected action.

## T4 Connect each asset type to the appropriate rights evidence

**Bonds and other securities:** identify the actual issuer and security, the record owner or entitlement holder, custody chain, transfer restrictions, and treatment of interest, principal, corporate actions, and defaults. A privately issued token referencing a bond may create a claim against the token issuer rather than a direct claim against the bond issuer. Document which relationship is intended.

**Safe Keeping Receipts:** establish precisely what the receipt acknowledges and whether any rights can be transferred. Tokenizing the receipt does not resolve whether its issuer holds the described asset or whether the depositor has transferable title. Keep receipt authentication and underlying asset verification as distinct findings.

**SBLCs:** obtain the issuing bank's authenticated undertaking and specialist review of beneficiary rights, transfer provisions, assignment of proceeds, expiry, and presentation conditions. A token cannot amend the bank's commitment or give its holder rights that the instrument and applicable law do not confer.

**Inherited assets:** verify the estate or trust representative's authority and the actual distribution or transfer required. A beneficiary's expected inheritance is not automatically authority to issue tokens over estate property.

**Gold and gems:** match each claim to identified items or a clearly documented pool, custody arrangement, authenticity and quality evidence, holder rights, and release process. Inspection of one item does not establish the composition of an entire pool.

These are routing questions for the relevant professional reviewers. The platform should not supply a generic legal template that purports to transfer every kind of asset across all jurisdictions.

## T5 Prevent duplicate and inconsistent claims

Use one canonical asset identifier linked to verified serials, registry references, custody lots, or security positions. Track all representations of that asset: direct holdings, receipts, tokens, wrapped versions, liens, collateral assignments, and pending redemptions. Review external claims as well as internal records; an internal uniqueness check cannot discover every pledge made elsewhere.

For a pool, keep a reconciliation ledger showing confirmed eligible backing, units outstanding, units reserved for pending settlement, and adjustments. Define the exact conversion method, rounding policy, fees, and treatment of shortfalls. Do not call backing fully available if it is also committed to an incompatible obligation.

When moving tokens across networks, document the lock, burn, mint, and release relationships. Prevent independent representations from being counted twice. A proof that two contracts each contain units does not prove that two sets of underlying assets exist.

For an indivisible object such as a gemstone, distinguish fractional economic interests from physical redemption rights. Multiple investors cannot each be promised exclusive delivery of the same stone. If sale proceeds are the intended benefit, say so and identify the rules for sale, expenses, valuation disputes, and distributions.

Recommended exception handling: suspend affected issuance when reconciliation fails, preserve the discrepancy, notify authorized reviewers, investigate, and record any corrective action. Do not quietly adjust backing numbers to fit the token supply. These controls are proposed product requirements rather than claims about an existing universal registry.

## T6 Design redemption and failure procedures

A credible asset-backed arrangement must explain the whole life of the claim. Ask what happens when a holder requests redemption, when a payment is late, when an asset is damaged, when a custodian fails, and when a private key is lost. Identify deadlines, required evidence, charges, eligible recipients, and dispute routes.

Reconcile burning or locking a token with release of the underlying right. Avoid a process in which the customer loses the token before the party responsible for delivery is bound to perform. Determine what happens if either leg fails and how records are corrected. Include the settlement asset in the review: a payment token introduces its own issuer, liquidity, and redemption questions.

Confirm whether insurance exists, who is insured, the covered risks, exclusions, limits, and claims process. The presence of an insurance certificate does not by itself establish that every holder's loss is covered. Obtain the insurer's or broker's appropriate confirmation through independently verified channels when insurance is material to the claim.

Document lost-key recovery and inheritance without silently replacing the legal owner. Identify who may request recovery, the evidence required, any waiting or objection period, the authorized decision maker, and the audit record. Show customers the consequences of administrator recovery powers.

A dissolution plan should identify who maintains ownership records, who continues servicing or redemption, how records can be exported securely, and what happens if the website or chain becomes unavailable. A token should not depend on the continued existence of a single marketing page for holders to understand their rights.

## T7 United States regulatory source status on November 3 2026

The legal knowledge base must distinguish a statute, Commission action, staff interpretation, and proposal. Preserve the exact source, publication date, effective date where stated, jurisdiction, and status.

| Verified source | Status and practical significance |
| --- | --- |
| SEC interpretation issued March 17, 2026; effective March 23, 2026 | Addresses categories of crypto assets and transactions. A tokenized security remains within securities analysis; an asset that is not itself a security can nevertheless be involved in an investment contract. [T02][T03] |
| SEC Division of Corporation Finance FAQs updated November 2, 2026 | Staff views addressing the interpretation, expressly without the force of a Commission rule. Apply the particular facts and limitations instead of using a label such as decentralized as a legal conclusion. [T04] |
| Regulation Crypto Assets issued August 18, 2026 | The official page identifies this as a proposed rule. Proposed offering exemptions and a proposed safe harbor must not be presented as presently available authorization. [T05] |
| SEC order issued September 17, 2026 | Temporary conditional relief concerns specified tokenized NMS-stock venues and liquidity providers. It is not blanket permission to operate an unrestricted marketplace for all tokenized assets. [T06] |

For a proposed Asset Machine transaction, qualified counsel should identify the actual asset and rights, relevant offering rules or exemptions, investor limitations, intermediary roles, custody duties, marketing restrictions, transfer conditions, and applicable state and foreign law. A software token name does not decide those issues.

Refresh this section before relying on it for an actual launch or transaction. A new proposal, filing, staff no-action position, or conditional order is not interchangeable with a general license. An attorney's dated analysis should identify its assumptions and the exact activity it covers.

## T8 Money transmission and sanctions require an activity review

FinCEN's 2019 convertible-virtual-currency guidance analyzes business models and underlying conduct. A platform that merely supplies information is different from a business accepting and transmitting value or operating a qualifying exchange or administration function. Classify what the business actually does, then evaluate applicable federal and state requirements with current counsel. Do not assume that every wallet connection creates money-transmitter status or that a noncustodial label automatically removes it. [T07]

OFAC's virtual-currency guidance applies sanctions considerations to digital transactions and describes risk-based screening, customer information, and ongoing review. A blockchain address may be identifying information in a sanctions record; context and the actual applicable prohibition matter. A vendor's risk score is not itself a legal determination, and a clean address search is not comprehensive clearance. [W09]

Asset Machine's proposed controls should preserve the screening date, list versions, matched identifiers, analyst resolution, relevant jurisdiction, and any restriction or exception. Human review should resolve uncertain identity matches and material alerts. Record evidence neutrally; an incidental transaction relationship alone should not be described as proof that a person is sanctioned or criminal.

## T9 Hashes and attestations prove limited things

A cryptographic hash can help demonstrate that a retrieved file matches the bytes previously recorded. It cannot establish that the original document was truthful, that a signature was authorized, or that the described asset exists. NIST's blockchain overview explains the external-data problem: a ledger can preserve information supplied by humans or sensors without establishing that the information matches reality. [T09]

A verifiable credential adds a way to check an issuer's signed claims and their status. W3C explicitly distinguishes checking the credential from determining whether its claims are true. Asset Machine still needs to evaluate the issuer, authority, scope, subject, dates, and evidence behind the statement. [T10]

An oracle makes external information available to a smart contract; it does not remove the need to trust and evaluate the sources. Record who supplies the information, how it is collected, how often it is updated, and what happens when it is missing or contradictory. Several feeds repeating the same original statement do not provide several independent inspections. [T11]

**Recommended evidence record:** case identifier; precise claim; source organization; independently verified contact; asset identifier; document hash; issue and observation dates; method; reviewer; result; exclusions; confidence explanation; expiry or review trigger; and supporting evidence references. Limit access to personal data and commercially sensitive documents. Publish the minimum useful status and scope, rather than identities, account records, or full estate documents on a permanent public chain.

## T10 Approved answer patterns for the knowledge assistant

The following are proposed response patterns. They prevent the assistant from turning a partial technical observation into a financial or legal certification.

| User question | Appropriate answer pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Is this wallet real? | Identify the chain and address, state what independently obtained data shows, and explain which control and ownership questions remain. |
| Does a successful signature prove ownership? | It supports the specified authorization check at a stated time. Legal ownership and permission to sell or pledge require separate evidence. |
| Can you verify my asset from this PDF? | Extract the claims, identify required independent confirmations, and classify what is documented versus still unconfirmed. |
| Can I tokenize it now? | Review the underlying asset, the holder's proposed legal right, the parties, custody, restrictions, code, and transaction requirements before issuing a readiness finding. |
| Does a token make an old bond redeemable? | No such conclusion follows from minting. Establish the original obligation and its current enforceability through the relevant issuer and legal review. |
| Is this proof of reserves enough? | Explain the exact report's scope and what it does not address, especially liabilities, rights, restrictions, and subsequent changes. |
| Will a bank lend against it? | Only the specific lender can decide under its underwriting and collateral rules; evidence of existence is not a financing commitment. |

Useful status language includes **claim received**, **document reviewed**, **independent confirmation pending**, **specified fact confirmed as of a stated time**, **material discrepancy**, and **specialist decision required**. State whose confirmation is being reported and what it covers. Avoid global labels such as guaranteed real, fraud-free, government approved, or fully liquid unless the exact claim is supported and authorized for publication.

An unknown result is a legitimate result. The assistant should name the missing evidence and the next responsible verifier, retain conflicting evidence, and route the case to the correct reviewer. It should never invent a bank confirmation, appraisal, chain query, legal opinion, or regulator approval to complete an attractive customer report.

# Asset Machine Operating Reference

This part defines recommended knowledge and review behavior for Asset Machine. It is an editorial and operational design, not a representation that these controls are deployed or that the platform is a licensed institution. External law and instrument terms still govern each case.

## O01 Answering a user without overstating the evidence

An answer should identify the asset category, explain the relevant concept, name the evidence required, identify an independent verification route, and state the most useful next step. When a user asks about a particular asset, distinguish their assertions from observed records and from conclusions supplied by an authorized reviewer.

Use the following answer structure for a case question:

1. **What the record says.** Quote only the necessary wording and identify the submitted evidence.
2. **What has been checked.** Name the actual check and its scope, time and source.
3. **What remains unknown.** List material gaps without implying that absence of evidence proves fraud.
4. **Who can resolve it.** Identify the relevant institution, official registry or qualified specialist.
5. **Next step.** Give one concrete evidence or review action.

For example: "The submitted receipt lists ten gold bars. I have not obtained confirmation from the named vault or matched the bars to a current inventory. An authorized vault officer can confirm the holding and applicable release terms through an independently established channel. The next step is a consented custody-confirmation request with the bar list."

Do not transform that answer into "Your gold is verified." A photograph of a document is evidence that the file contains a claim. It is not the underlying institution's confirmation of that claim.

## O02 Public education and private case review

The public knowledge base should contain general explanations, synthetic examples, checklists, source links and safe starting questions. It should not expose a user's case, bank account, estate file, identity documents, wallet history linked to their identity, unpublished valuation, privileged work or reviewer notes.

The private assistant may use only the records and permissions of the authenticated case. It should reference the specific evidence version used. A general article must not be treated as evidence that a particular asset passed a check. An uploaded document must not be allowed to instruct the assistant to ignore review requirements or disclose another customer's data.

Any case conclusion should retain its scope. A released report saying "ownership could not be established" is a completed report, not a verified ownership result. Public examples should not be populated from real private matters without an approved privacy review and permission appropriate to the intended disclosure.

## O03 Voice answers and deeper reading

Keep the first spoken explanation concise, normally about 60 to 110 words. Offer the relevant detailed article, evidence checklist and source links in text. Expand when the visitor asks a specific follow-up. Do not read a long legal or technical chapter aloud by default.

Clarify ambiguity. "Do you mean a gold bar held by a vault, or a token that claims to represent gold?" avoids answering a token-rights question with only a metals-assay explanation. Similarly, "Are you applying for an SBLC, reviewing one you received, or considering a financing offer?" determines the correct pathway.

For the terms SKR and safe keeping receipt, define the intended meaning explicitly. If a different industry uses SKR differently, ask for the document wording. For a wallet question, request only the public chain and address needed for the permitted task. Never request recovery phrases or private keys in speech, text or uploads.

If a question falls outside the reviewed knowledge, say that the library does not establish the answer and identify the source or professional needed. Do not fill the gap with a fabricated bank practice, legal exemption, valuation or product category.

## O04 Claims the assistant may and may not make

| Situation | Appropriate wording | Overstatement to prevent |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer response received | "The issuer confirmed the listed obligation remains outstanding as of the response date, subject to the stated qualifications." | "Guaranteed cash equivalent." |
| Report number located | "The laboratory's records contain this report number; physical-item matching is a separate check." | "The stone is authentic because the number exists." |
| Custody verified | "The custodian confirmed the identified holding and disclosed the following limitations." | "No liens exist anywhere." |
| Wallet signature verified | "The challenge was validated for this address under the checked signing rules at this time." | "This proves legal ownership and clean funds." |
| Token code inspected | "The reviewed version contains these permissions and transfer restrictions." | "The contract is completely secure." |
| Valuation supplied | "The appraiser estimated this type of value on this date, with these assumptions." | "You can sell or borrow this amount today." |
| No adequate response | "We could not establish this claim through the documented checks." | "This person is a criminal." |
| Financing discussed | "The institution supplied an indicative proposal with conditions." | "Funding is approved." |

These examples prescribe careful language. They do not authorize the system to claim that any check actually occurred.

## O05 Source governance and corrections

Every article needs a stable identifier, topic, version, research date, source references, jurisdiction scope and editorial state. For legal content, retain the type of authority: statute, regulation, binding order, interpretation, staff guidance, proposal, court decision or industry standard. Do not silently present a proposal as effective law.

Record the publication or update date when available, the retrieval date, and whether the complete authoritative page or only a search excerpt was accessible. If a source cannot be reached, preserve that limitation. A dead link is not evidence that the underlying rule has been repealed, and an old page still being online does not establish that it remains controlling.

Recommended maintenance policy: review fast-changing legal and digital-asset content before consequential reliance and whenever a known rule changes; periodically review other educational pages; refresh transaction facts at their relevant point of reliance. This is a proposed policy, not a scheduled task created by this pack.

Corrections should preserve the prior version, explain the change, update affected cross-references and remove outdated wording from active retrieval. If a released private report relied materially on a changed factual finding, route it to the responsible reviewer under the existing permission and notification policy. Do not automatically email affected people without authorization.

## O06 A practical cost policy for knowledge answers

Use the existing guides and local search first. Store one canonical article and reuse it for web display, downloads and voice summaries. Do not paste the entire knowledge base into every model request. Retrieve only the few relevant sections and their source metadata.

Use deterministic checks for dates, missing fields, duplicate identifiers, source links, hashes, unit conversions and clearly defined workflow states. These checks should produce factual observations, not unaudited authenticity scores. Use existing local inference only when interpretation is needed and within the actual deployment's permissions and cost controls.

Research can often begin with no-fee official sources. Professional examinations, bank services, shipping and legal review may still have legitimate costs. The platform should describe any proposed service and obtain the required consent before charging. The availability of prepaid infrastructure or model credits does not prove the entire workflow is free.

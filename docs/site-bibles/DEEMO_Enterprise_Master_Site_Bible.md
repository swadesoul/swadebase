## Arthur Mitchell and Harold Whaley: the dramatic engine (founder's firsthand direction)
Harold describes Arthur as the older, commercially oriented partner. Arthur is quick to identify a money-making or relationship opportunity and tries to move a deal forward. Harold, the younger tech-oriented partner, pushes Arthur to embrace emerging technologies, is quicker to challenge assumptions, and tends to identify the flaw or risk in an attractive deal. Harold can become combative, but when Arthur explains something that makes practical sense Harold changes his mind. The men are like an odd couple: divergent instincts, fast teasing, genuine frustration, mutual respect, laughter, long familiarity, and loyalty. Neither is always right and neither is the other's sidekick. Arthur must not be flattened into anti-technology; Harold must not be flattened into anti-money. Their disagreements are intelligent, specific, affectionate, and consequential.

## Mandatory performance direction for the show
Dialogue is natural, vivid, expressive, conversational, and often animated. Include quick challenges, meaningful overlaps or interruptions, variable rhythm, vocal emphasis, pointed questions, subtle side glances, half-smiles, head turns, expressive hands, warm teasing, laughter, dramatic silences, and credible shifts from tension to understanding. Do not robotically read dialogue. Show characters actually listening and reacting when off-camera. Expressiveness varies with context and does not justify constant yelling or invented historical quotations.

## Pilot Scene 1 — locked original text, dramatized not verbatim
INT. PRIVATE OFFICE — PRESENT DAY. Arthur and Harold discuss space-economy financing; screen shows business presentation. Arthur asks how they will finance the opportunity. Harold starts describing the technology. Arthur interrupts:
ARTHUR: "You still doing the same thing you were doing thirty years ago. Building something before anybody knows what to do with it."
HAROLD: "And you're still figuring out how to get somebody to pay for it."
They laugh. Arthur becomes thoughtful.
ARTHUR: "You remember when we were sharing that car?"
HAROLD: "Man, you tore that car up."
Flashback: a battered car enters a 1990s San Diego parking lot.
TITLE: DEEMO — AHEAD OF THE FUTURE.

## Scene beats
1. Arthur eyes the projected budget, leans in, asks the money question with an amused but serious challenge. Harold is energized by the technology and sees the deal's structural weakness.
2. Arthur interrupts with an affectionate provocation. Harold holds his look, smiles skeptically and lands the money retort. Their overlap should feel practiced after decades together.
3. Both laugh genuinely, then Arthur's expression softens. An almost imperceptible pause precedes the car memory; Harold answers with playful accusation and warmth.
4. Car flashback shifts to 1990s San Diego. Keep character appearance, wardrobe, vehicles, businesses and period setting historically researched.
